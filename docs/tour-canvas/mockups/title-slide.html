The title slide, three ways

Today the title slide is the words on the left and a faint ribbon on the right: half the slide says nothing about what the lab does. Each direction below keeps the words exactly as they are, holds them still so any photo catches them whole, and gives the right half a loop of real bioinformatics: DNA read into data, reads assembled into a genome, or genomes compared on a tree.

Each slide is live, at the canvas's real 1280 × 720 layout. With reduced motion turned on in your system, they show their still version.

What it looks like now

A

DNA becomes data

The story

A double helix turns down the right side. As its base pairs swing to the front, letters peel off and fly into a FASTQ read that types itself out: the moment a sequencer turns a sample into data.

What moves

  • The helix turns once every 12 seconds; each base pair swings forward and back, its rung flipping edge-on.
  • A, C, G, T fly into the read every 9 seconds.
  • The read types itself, base by base.

Still version

The helix and a finished read, with nothing flying. The bases keep the tour's colours: A mint, C sky, G gold, T pink.

B

Reads become a genome

Recommended

The story

What the lab does every day, as a genome browser: short reads slide in and pile up under the reference, a scan calls each base of the consensus, and one column lights up as a variant, C → T, seen in 6 of 7 reads. It says "raw sequence data into answers" without a word.

What moves

  • Ten reads glide in, a scan line calls the 24 bases, the variant and its note appear; the loop runs 16 seconds.
  • The 240 cores twinkle as jobs run; the 360 TB gauge fills.
  • A genome map turns slowly in the corner, once every 90 seconds.

Why recommended

It is the one picture here that only a bioinformatics lab would show, and it makes the two facts live: the cores look busy and the storage fills. A turning helix says "DNA"; this says "we analyse it".

C

Genomes on a tree

The story

A time-scaled tree of virus genomes grows from its root, branch by branch, as the lab's COVID-19 surveillance built it. A line sweeps through time and each genome flashes as it is reached. It previews the Variant tree slide later in the tour.

What moves

  • Branches draw out from the root over five seconds, oldest first; each tip pops as its branch arrives.
  • A time line sweeps 2021 to 2023, lighting the tips it passes.
  • The tree fades and regrows every 14 seconds.

Still version

The whole tree, drawn, with its lineage labels. It fades behind the words on the left so the title stays clear.

The same in all three

  • The words don't change. Title, intro and the two facts are today's, in the same place and size. They rise once as the slide opens (about 1.5 seconds) and then hold still.
  • The white PGC Visayas logo joins "Welcome to the lab tour" at the top, so the slide names the lab in Present mode too, where the page's header is hidden.
  • Motion uses only movement and fading, pauses when the slide is off screen, and stops for visitors who turn motion off: they see the still version.
  • Colours are the tour's own: the base colours from the presentation decks, on the dark gradient the title slide has now.
